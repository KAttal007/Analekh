Analekh Architecture

The analyst’s record, kept for you.

Every NSE-listed company is preloaded. Its results are fetched from NSE’s integrated filings and, back to March 2023, its financial-results list, then parsed and validated on both bases for every filer. Closing prices come once a day from NSE’s bhavcopy, and board meetings from NSE’s feeds fill a results calendar. For a company you follow, the system will find and update your Excel model after you approve each change, or build a new model from your sector template, and draft a quarterly update or an initiating coverage note in Word and PowerPoint. Core numbers come from XBRL; the AI writes the analysis and pulls every figure from the database.

Results come from NSE’s integrated-filing lookup (per company, and one request every 2 hours without a ticker for the latest filings) and its financial-results list for 2023–2024. When a quarter is refiled, the most recently filed values are used and the older ones kept as history. Sectors follow NSE’s own taxonomy first. Prices: one bhavcopy per trading day; only the latest close is kept. Celery beat runs every job in Docker. Only the relevant PDF pages will reach Claude, and nothing is written to Excel until the analyst approves.

Workflow 1 · Results collection built

Four triggers, two NSE lookups, one pipeline. The watcher asks once, without a ticker, for the latest filings across all companies; a refresh or backfill asks per company. The history backfill reads the financial-results list, whose files are complete from March 2023. Each file is downloaded once (SHA-256, stored in MinIO); files missing their main contexts are rejected and a broken file is retried once, then skipped, so one bad file never costs a company its other filings. Values are inserted as history and the newest filed value of each line becomes the one in use, whatever order files arrived in. One transaction per company: a stopped run keeps what it stored and resumes at the company it stopped on. NSE pauses after a few hundred to a few thousand requests; runs wait 10 minutes and go on.

Workflow 2 · Daily closing prices built

One file per trading day, after the close. NSE publishes the bhavcopy in the evening; the job tries hourly from 19:00 to 23:00 IST on weekdays until the day is stored, then does nothing. Market cap is the close times shares in issue, worked out from the latest results filing (paid-up equity capital divided by face value). Only the latest close is kept (owner’s rule, 18 October 2026): each file replaces the previous day’s prices, and the day’s change comes from the previous close the file carries. No live prices, no price history.

Workflow 3 · Company list and sectors built

Every NSE company, then its sector. The equity and SME lists are loaded weekly. Sectors follow NSE’s own taxonomy (22 sectors) from NSE Indices’ published constituent lists, which outrank the LSEG industry groups imported from an Excel Stocks data type workbook; a user’s value will outrank both. The LSEG group is always kept in its own column. The results format (Ind-AS, bank, NBFC, general or life insurer) is first guessed from strong signals only, then decided by the line items each filing tags.

Workflow 4 · Opening Analekh and reading results built

Welcome, then the desk. The first visit of a browser session to the home page shows the welcome screen with live counts; a scroll, swipe or tap unlocks it into the Results desk and its results calendar. Search runs in the browser over the whole universe. On a company page, the results history shares one request with the tiles and the chart; each quarter’s heading links its filing on NSE (iXBRL, the newest after a refiling) and opens its details. Data stays fresh in the browser for five minutes, so moving between tabs shows it at once. Motion is only on what you click; figures appear immediately.

Workflow 5 · Quarterly model update planned · phase 2

Steps 3 and 4 run only when the filing isn’t already in the database (for most companies it is, collected by workflow 1). The linked model is found through watched folders or the open workbook, identified by a hidden tag, its file name or its contents. Steps 6 and 7 are the approval gate: the analyst sees which cells change and which forecasts need rolling forward, accepts or edits each one, and only then is a new model version saved. The original workbook is never overwritten.

What changed in v0.0.5

  • Unit check (V11): amounts tagged in the wrong unit corrected when two signals agree; 28 corrected, 44 suspects held for a PDF check.
  • Scheduler: Celery beat in Docker; the watcher follows SEBI’s seasons; the feed watcher runs.
  • Results calendar: from board meeting intimations, on the desk and the watchlist.
  • Company page: results history whose quarter headings link each filing; the filings box and raw XBRL links are gone.
  • Reports: set up a quarterly update or an initiating coverage note (Word and dense PowerPoint); drafting next.
  • Ratings: read from the analyst’s own model, never generated (owner’s amendment).

Workflow 6 · Start coverage planned · phase 2

Used when no model exists yet. The analyst picks a company and a sector template (their own upload or a built-in one). The builder fills the template’s inputs with sourced history and keeps its formulas. Proposed assumptions pass through the same approval gate before the tagged model is saved. From the next results onward, the quarterly update takes over.

Workflow 7 · Template onboarding planned · phase 2

Confirm once per template. The workbook understanding engine reads any workbook: its period axis, inputs versus formulas, row labels, drivers, checks and the formula graph. It suggests where each standard line goes, with a confidence for each row; the analyst confirms or fixes it once. A test build with a real company must pass (checks hold, no broken formulas) before the template can be used.

Workflow 8 · Documents, KPIs and reports reports set-up built drafting · phases 3–4 · live calls: future scope

Only selected pages reach Claude, and every number is checked. For watchlist or on-demand companies, PDFs are read locally, pages classified and only the relevant ones sent for extraction against a JSON schema, each value with its page and quote. XBRL wins over any AI value. The analyst sets up a quarterly update or an initiating coverage note on the Reports screen (company, basis, quarter; built). Drafts are written in a tool loop over the database, the comps and the model; a grounding check blocks any number that did not come from a tool result or a calculation, and a style check blocks em-dashes and other marks of generated text. The rating comes from the analyst’s model or the analyst, never from Analekh. Future scope: a listener that transcribes the earnings call live.

Workflow 9 · Events, results calendar and alerts feeds · calendar built red flags · alerts · phase 5

NSE’s published event feeds. The feed watcher runs from Celery beat and keeps every item. Companies must announce a results board meeting at least two working days ahead (SEBI LODR 29), so the Board Meetings feed now builds the results calendar on the desk and the watchlist; a past day shows who actually filed. Next: telling the results watcher which days matter, and alerts for watchlist companies when they file results or when a red flag appears (pledges, insider trades, related-party deals, auditor changes).

When each job runs built · celery beat in docker

Results arrive in four seasons. SEBI requires quarterly results within 45 days of the quarter (60 for the year end). NSE showed integrated filings a median of one second after the company filed (299 filings, 24 companies), so any delay is the watcher’s interval. Since 2023, every first filing of those companies fell between day 8 and day 59, inside the windows; under 1% were filed between midnight and 09:00. The watcher polls every 2 hours from 09:00 to 23:00 in season and once a day at 21:00 otherwise: about 60% fewer requests than every 2 hours all year.

Data rights

NSE’s published RSS feeds and lists, two lookups (owner-approved; per company, plus one request every 2 hours), one bhavcopy a day, NSE Indices’ constituent CSVs. Requests identify as Analekh, never as a browser. Written confirmation or a licence before any public release.
